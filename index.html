Reading rules

On vs kun

On’yomi (音読み) is the Chinese-derived sound. Kun’yomi (訓読み) is the native Japanese word the kanji was matched to. One kanji usually has both: 食 is ショク (on) in 洋食 but た-べる (kun) in 食べ放題.

Rules of thumb

  • Kanji + trailing hiragana (okurigana) → kun: 焼く, 辛い, 食べる.
  • Two or more kanji together → usually on + on: 軟骨 なんこつ, 洋食 ようしょく, 禁煙席 きんえんせき.
  • A lone kanji noun is often kun (塩 しお, 魚 さかな, 網 あみ) but not always: 肉 にく and 茶 ちゃ are on. Check each one.
  • Native food words are often kun + kun: 刺身 さしみ, 手羽先 てばさき, 赤身 あかみ.

Mixed readings

  • Kun + on (湯桶読み, yutō-yomi): 家系 いえ+けい, 梅酒 うめ+しゅ, 熱燗 あつ+かん.
  • On + kun (重箱読み, jūbako-yomi): 特盛り とく+もり, 税込 ぜい+こみ.

Okurigana

The kana after a kanji marks which kun reading it is, and the form of the word. In 少ない the kanji is only すく; ない is okurigana. In 柔らかい the kanji is やわ; らかい is okurigana. Nouns made from verb stems often drop or keep it: 焼き鳥, 持ち帰り, 刺身 (刺し), 帆立 (立て).

Rendaku (voicing)

The first sound of the second part often becomes voiced: た→だ, さ→ざ, こ→ご, は→ば, ふ→ぶ, き→ぎ, す→ず. Examples: 替えだま (玉), 焼きざかな (魚), 穴ご (子), 手ばさき (羽), 砂ぎも (肝), 鰹ぶし (節), かきごおり (氷), 回転ずし (寿司). Common in kun + kun compounds, not guaranteed.

Sound changes in on readings

  • Small っ: ク / ツ / チ / キ at the end of an on reading becomes っ before K, S, T, CH, P sounds: 食券 しょっけん, 抹茶 まっちゃ, 鉄板 てっぱん, 納豆 なっとう.
  • H → P after ん or っ: 単品 たんぴん (ヒン), 鉄板 てっぱん (ハン).

Whole-word readings (熟字訓)

Some words are read as a unit; you can’t map kanji to sounds one by one. Learn the whole word: 海老 えび, 烏賊 いか, 南瓜 かぼちゃ, 茄子 なす, 蕎麦 そば, 海苔 のり, 木耳 きくらげ, 味噌 みそ, 生姜 しょうが, 雲丹 うに, 山葵 わさび, 寿司 すし (ateji: kanji chosen for sound or luck).

Loanword readings

餃子 ぎょうざ and 叉焼 チャーシュー arrived from Chinese together with the word, so they don’t follow Japanese on/kun. 烏龍 ウーロン too.

One kanji, many readings

Context decides. 辛い is からい (spicy) or つらい (painful). 生 is なま in 生ビール, せい in 生活, しょう in 一生. 鮭 and 酒 are both さけ, but they are different kanji.
